# Genre guidance

Apply the common rules without flattening the genre.

## Technical documentation

Optimize for an unblocked reader. Keep exact commands, versions, identifiers, expected output, and ordered steps. Lists and term-definition entries are legitimate when they make scanning easier. Remove unsupported performance claims, decorative headings, and marketing language.

## Technical reports and analyses

Assume a mixed technical audience with general engineering knowledge but no access to the discussion that produced the document. Lead with a plain summary of the problem and conclusion, then provide the context and technical depth needed to support it. Make actors, components, prerequisites, evidence, relationships, consequences, and requested actions explicit. Use the [comprehension pass](comprehension.md) for complex, multi-paragraph documents.

## Reference, API, and policy material

Optimize for accurate retrieval and predictable scanning. Keep repeated schemas, definitions, tables, normative language, caveats, links, and consistent entry structure. Do not vary a useful template merely to make it look less machine-generated.

## Email

Answer early. Keep enough warmth for the relationship and the existing thread. Remove generic greetings, repeated context, and padded closing offers when they do not serve the recipient.

## Marketing and product copy

Lead with the concrete benefit, mechanism, audience, or supplied proof. Remove generic superlatives, inflated importance, and feature lists that repeat the same claim. Do not invent customer outcomes, benchmarks, or urgency.

## Academic and safety-sensitive writing

Preserve calibrated hedges, citations, defined terms, logical qualifiers, warnings, conditions, escalation paths, and conventional structure. Passive voice may be appropriate. Do not simplify legal, medical, safety, or policy material by removing uncertainty, attribution, scope, protections, or traceable authority. Treat protected legal and policy text as immutable.

## User-interface text

Optimize for task completion, brevity, accessibility, error recovery, and consistent product terminology. Preserve labels, constraints, localization requirements, and product semantics. Do not add personality when it obscures the action or changes meaning.

## Social posts, blogs, and newsletters

Keep a real point of view, useful digressions, and voice that exists in the source. Remove engagement bait, fake revelation, template cadence, and generic summaries. Do not create slang, personality, or personal experience that the author did not provide.
